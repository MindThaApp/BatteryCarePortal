Four decisions this design needs from the owner

  1. Make the basic charge limit free. Laptop makers already give the on/off limit away, and Power Plan Manager sold no Pro add-ons in twelve months with the limit behind Pro. Pro should sell what the makers do not offer: top-up, schedules, heat protection, rules and history.
  2. Launch on Lenovo IdeaPad and Yoga only. Every other laptop opens in an honest Monitor mode that still gives health, power flow, history and reminders.
  3. Sell Pro only where it does something. On a laptop with no working hardware limit the app shows what Pro would add there and does not push it.
  4. Rename "sailing" to "Rest range" in the interface. New users do not know the term. Keep "sailing" as a search word in Settings and the Store text.

1Pitch, target users and positioning

One line: Keep your laptop battery healthy. Set it once, see that it works, pay once.

A lithium-ion battery ages faster when it sits full and warm. Most laptops live on a desk, plugged in at 100%. The app fixes that one habit and shows the result in the tray.

Desk-docked owner (main)

Laptop on a charger or dock most of the week. Wants the battery to still be good in year four. Needs one switch and a visible "it is working".

Hybrid traveller

Plugged in at home, on the road on Fridays. Needs a full battery on travel days without remembering to switch anything. This user buys Pro for scheduled top-up.

Careful buyer

Paid a lot for the laptop and reads about battery wear. Wants numbers: health, cycles, temperature, watts, history. Distrusts big claims.

Out of scope: desktops, custom fan curves, overclocking, and gamers who want deep hardware tuning. Those users belong to Power Plan Manager.

Free versus Pro

Free, for good

  • Device check and honest support status
  • Health, cycles, temperature, live watts
  • Power flow
  • Charge limit on or off, at the level the laptop supports
  • Unplug at the limit and plug in at 20% reminders
  • 24 hours of history
  • Restore laptop defaults and the diagnostics report. Safety is never paid.

Pro, one payment, 14-day trial

  • Top up to 100% once, and scheduled top-up
  • Heat protection
  • Any-level limit and rest range, where the laptop supports them
  • Calibration wizard and reminder
  • Battery rules
  • 30-day history, health trend, export
  • Tray icon styles and cooling modes (later)

Suggested price: one payment near US$9.99, tested between $6.99 and $12.99. Pro features stay visible in the free app with a small Pro pill and work in read-only form, as they do today.

3Device-support UX

The app checks the laptop with read-only calls on first launch and puts it in one of five states. Every state is a finished screen with something useful on it. The app never shows a greyed-out page of controls that cannot work.

StateWhat the user seesWhat works
Supported"Charge limit works on this laptop." Lenovo IdeaPad and Yoga with the maker's energy-management driver.Everything. On these models the limit is an on/off switch that holds near 60% (80% on some), so the control is a switch, not a slider.
On the test list"Monitor mode. Your Dell is on our test list. The charge limit stays off until it is confirmed on this model." Button: Help test it.Health, power flow, history, reminders. Dell, HP, ASUS, ThinkPad.
Monitor mode"This laptop does not offer a charge limit that the app can use safely. You still get health, power flow, history and an unplug reminder at the level you pick."Health, power flow, history, reminders.

No battery"This PC has no battery. Battery Care is for laptops." Link to Power Plan Manager. No empty pages behind it.Nothing else is shown.

Rules for Monitor mode

  • Hardware-only cards are replaced by one explanation card. They are not shown disabled.
  • The reminder says what it is: "The app can remind you. It cannot stop the charger on this laptop, and it cannot act while the PC sleeps."
  • No Pro purchase prompt unless a Pro feature works on this laptop.
  • If another tool changes the limit, the app says so and asks: keep the new value, or put the app's value back.

Beta-tester path for unverified brands

  1. Opt in. Settings, Device support, "Help test charge limit on Dell laptops". Off by default, per device.
  2. Read-only check. The app lists the firmware settings it found and their current values. Nothing is written.
  3. Plain consent. What will change, by name. How to undo it: Restore in the app, or the firmware setup screen. A firmware password stops the test.
  4. One write, read back. If the value does not read back, the app restores the saved original at once. It never retries.
  5. Watch and report. A "Test in progress" chip stays on the limit card until the app sees charging stop at the limit. The tester then reviews a short report (model, firmware version, method, result; no serial number) and chooses to copy or send it.

ASUS firmware cannot report the current limit, so its card keeps the note "the app cannot confirm this value" even after verification.

4Information architecture and navigation

The tray is the product. Most days the user only looks at the icon. The flyout handles nine tasks out of ten. The window is for setup, numbers and history.

Tray icon

  • Battery glyph with fill level and a tick at the limit
  • Colour and shape change with the state
  • Tooltip: "60% · Holding at limit · Health 91.7%"
  • Left click: flyout
  • Right click: Top up to 100% once, Turn limit off until tomorrow, Open, Settings, Quit

Flyout (main surface)

  • Level, state sentence, limit tick
  • Charge limit control
  • Top up to 100%
  • One line of power flow
  • Health and temperature footer
  • Open Battery Care

Window

  • Overview: status, health, what happened today
  • Charging: limit, rest range, top-up, schedule, reminders
  • Heat protection
  • Power flow
  • History: level, watts, health trend
  • Settings: startup, tray, notifications, device support, safety, Pro, diagnostics

Six items in a left NavigationView, Settings pinned at the bottom as Windows users expect. Rules and Calibration join Charging in 1.1 as cards, so the navigation does not grow. Closing the window keeps the app in the tray, and the first close says so once.

7Visual direction

Fluent, quiet, mostly neutral. Mica window background, standard WinUI controls, Segoe UI Variable, 4 and 8 px corner radii, Segoe Fluent Icons. Custom drawing is limited to three things: the battery glyph with its tick, the ring, and the power-flow lines.

One idea repeats everywhere: a fill and a tick. Tray icon, flyout bar, ring and slider all show level against limit in the same way.

Type. Display weight for the level and the page title only. Numbers use tabular figures so they do not jump as they update.

Motion. A slow drift on power-flow lines and a gentle fill change. Nothing pulses or blinks. All motion stops when Windows animation effects are off.

Health never turns red. A battery at 78% health is a fact shown in neutral text with one line of plain advice.

Accessibility

  • State is always a word plus a shape. Colour only supports it. The tray icon reads by fill length and tick in high-contrast themes.
  • Text contrast of at least 4.5:1 in both themes. State colours have separate light and dark values and are checked on their own backgrounds.
  • Everything works from the keyboard: Win+B to the tray, Enter opens the flyout, Tab order runs top to bottom, Esc closes.
  • Narrator reads full sentences: "Battery 60 percent, holding at the limit of 60 percent, plugged in." The slider announces both limit and rest range. Charts have a table view.
  • Layout holds at 200% text size and in right-to-left languages. The battery glyph keeps its direction, as it does in Windows.
  • Toasts are few: at most one per event, with no sound by default.

8Safety and trust

What happens if the app quits

The charge limit stays. It is stored in the laptop's firmware. It keeps working when the app is closed, when the laptop sleeps or is shut down, and after the app is uninstalled. To remove it, use Restore laptop defaults first.

The timed features stop. Top-up, schedules, heat protection, reminders and history need the app running in the tray.

EventCharge limitWhat the app does
Window closedStaysKeeps running in the tray. Says so once.
Quit from the trayStaysIf a top-up or calibration is running, the app puts the limit back before it exits and says so. This is new work: today the engine only stops polling.
Crash or forced close during a top-upOff until the app starts againOn next start the app sees the unfinished top-up, puts the limit back and writes a line in Today. Start with Windows covers restarts.
Sleep, hibernate, shut downStaysOn wake the app reads the value and re-applies it if the firmware lost it.
UninstallStaysA Store app cannot run code at uninstall. Settings and the Store description both say: restore defaults first.
Monitor mode (reminders only)None existsThe app cannot act while the PC sleeps, and warns before sleep if the level is above the chosen reminder level.
Say
  • "A lower charge level and less heat slow battery ageing."
  • "Health is your battery's own estimate. It can move a little day to day."
  • "About 25 min (estimate)."
  • "Your laptop does not report this."
  • "Calibration helps the gauge show the right percentage. It does not repair a battery."
Never say
  • "Doubles your battery life" or any number for years gained.
  • "Your battery is damaged" or "at risk".
  • Health scores, grades or red warnings invented by the app.
  • "Stops charging" on a laptop where the app can only remind.
  • Countdown timers, urgency or fear in any Pro prompt.

Engineering rules that carry the trust

  • Use only the interface the laptop maker's own software uses. No kernel drivers shipped, no embedded-controller writes, no undocumented pokes on unverified hardware.
  • Read before every write, save the original values once, read back after, never retry a failed write.
  • An administrator prompt appears only for a change the user just asked for, at most once per change. Background changes never prompt.
  • One owner for the limit. If Power Plan Manager or the maker's utility changes it, the app shows the new value and asks.
  • No account, no tracking. Nothing leaves the PC unless the user copies a report or turns on crash reports.
